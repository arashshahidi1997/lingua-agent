# Saʿdi — Bani Adam (Children of Adam)

## Summary

The most famous quatrain in Persian, from Saʿdi's *Golestan* (1258). A line of
it is woven into a carpet at the United Nations. Grammatically it is a compact
tour of **classical** Persian: the enclitic copula `-and`, chained ezafe, and
the poetic subordinator `چو`. Advanced comprehensible input — read for the
music first, then unpack.

## Source text

<div dir="rtl">

بنی‌آدم اعضای یکدیگرند
که در آفرینش ز یک گوهرند

چو عضوی به درد آورد روزگار
دگر عضوها را نماند قرار

</div>

_Textual note:_ a widely-recited variant reads
<span dir="rtl">اعضای یک پیکرند</span> _(a'zâ-ye yek peykar-and, "members of one
**body**")_ in the first line; the critical (Foruughi) text has
<span dir="rtl">یکدیگرند</span> _("of one **another**")_, used here.

## Transliteration

> Bani-âdam aʿzâ-ye yekdigar-and
> ke dar âfarinesh ze yek gowhar-and
> cho ʿozvi be dard âvarad ruzegâr
> degar ʿozvhâ râ namânad qarâr

## Bilingual reading

| fa | en (literal) |
|---|---|
| <span dir="rtl">بنی‌آدم اعضای یکدیگرند</span> | The children of Adam are members of one another, |
| <span dir="rtl">که در آفرینش ز یک گوهرند</span> | for in creation they are of one essence. |
| <span dir="rtl">چو عضوی به درد آورد روزگار</span> | When fate brings one member to pain, |
| <span dir="rtl">دگر عضوها را نماند قرار</span> | the other members cannot stay at rest. |

**Literary rendering:** _"Human beings are members of a whole, / in creation of
one essence and soul. / If one member is afflicted with pain, / other members
uneasy will remain."_ (after Iraj Bashiri's much-quoted verse translation)

## Vocabulary

<div dir="rtl">

- **بنی‌آدم** _(bani-âdam)_ — humankind, "the children of Adam"
- **اعضا** _(aʿzâ)_ — members, limbs _(singular عضو ʿozv)_
- **یکدیگر** _(yekdigar)_ — one another
- **آفرینش** _(âfarinesh)_ — creation
- **گوهر** _(gowhar)_ — essence, jewel, substance
- **روزگار** _(ruzegâr)_ — time, fate, "the age"
- **قرار** _(qarâr)_ — rest, repose, stability

</div>

## Grammar focus

- **Enclitic copula `-and`** — classical Persian attaches "they are" as a
  suffix: `یکدیگرند` = `یکدیگر + اند` ("[they] are of one another"). Modern prose
  would more often write `هستند`.
    - _evidence:_ <span dir="rtl">اعضای یکدیگرند / گوهرند</span>
- **Poetic `چو` (= چون)** — "when / as / since", clause-initial, common in verse
  for metre.
    - _evidence:_ <span dir="rtl">چو عضوی به درد آورد …</span>
- **Archaic negative subjunctive `نماند`** _(namânad)_ — "would not remain";
  the `نـ…ـد` frame on the present stem of ماندن, a classical form.
    - _evidence:_ <span dir="rtl">دگر عضوها را نماند قرار</span>
- **`را` marking the affected noun** — here it flags `عضوها` ("the other
  members") as the thing that "finds no rest".
