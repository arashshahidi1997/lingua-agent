# The ezafe — Persian's invisible linker

## Summary

The **ezafe** (اضافه) is the single most characteristic feature of Persian noun
phrases: an unstressed `-e` vowel (`-ye` after a vowel) added to a word to link
it to whatever modifies or possesses it. It is usually **not written** — you
hear it and infer it — which makes it the thing beginners most often miss. This
unit builds it from one noun up to a full chain.

## How it works

Persian puts the **head noun first**, then hangs modifiers and the possessor
after it, each joined by ezafe. English does the opposite (adjective before
noun; possessor with _'s_ or _of_).

| structure | Persian | translit | English |
|---|---|---|---|
| noun + possessor | <span dir="rtl">کتابِ من</span> | ketâb**-e** man | my book (book-_e_ me) |
| noun + adjective | <span dir="rtl">کتابِ بزرگ</span> | ketâb**-e** bozorg | a big book |
| noun + adj + possessor | <span dir="rtl">کتابِ بزرگِ من</span> | ketâb**-e** bozorg**-e** man | my big book |
| noun + noun (of) | <span dir="rtl">دانشگاهِ تهران</span> | dâneshgâh**-e** Tehrân | the University of Tehran |
| after a vowel → `-ye` | <span dir="rtl">خانه‌ی من</span> | khâne**-ye** man | my house |

## The two spelling cases

Ezafe is invisible **except** in two situations, which is where the writing
system helps you:

1. **After a final vowel**, ezafe surfaces as ی: <span dir="rtl">خانه‌ی بزرگ</span>
   _(khâne-ye bozorg)_, "a big house". You will also see the bare form
   <span dir="rtl">خانهٔ</span> with a small ٔ (hamze) on top — same sound.
2. **On a word ending in silent ه**, the same ی/ٔ appears:
   <span dir="rtl">نامهٔ من</span> _(nâme-ye man)_, "my letter".

Everywhere else it is a sound with no letter — so the diacritic `ِ` you see in
this lesson (as in <span dir="rtl">کتابِ</span>) is a **teaching aid**; native
text omits it.

## Grammar focus

- **Head-first, then modifiers, all chained by ezafe** — the chain can be
  arbitrarily long, and every link is the same `-e`.
    - _evidence:_ <span dir="rtl">ماشینِ قرمزِ گران‌قیمتِ پدرِ من</span> _(mâshin-e ghermez-e gerânghymat-e pedar-e man — "my father's expensive red car")_
- **Ezafe ≠ the indefinite `-i`** — `کتابی` _(ketâb-i)_ is "a book / some book",
  a different suffix. Don't confuse the ezafe `-e` with the indefinite `-i`.
    - _evidence:_ <span dir="rtl">کتابِ من (my book) ≠ کتابی (a book)</span>
- **Why phrases, not words, matter for substitution** — because agreement and
  possession ride on the ezafe chain, tools that swap vocabulary (the diglot
  reader) must substitute the **whole ezafe phrase**, or the link breaks. See
  `04_diglot_weave_preview_en_fa.md`.

## Exercises

1. **[translate_a_to_b]** "my big book"
    - _expected:_ <span dir="rtl">کتابِ بزرگِ من</span>
2. **[translate_a_to_b]** "the University of Tehran"
    - _expected:_ <span dir="rtl">دانشگاهِ تهران</span>
3. **[cloze]** Add the correct ezafe form after a vowel: <span dir="rtl">خانه‌ــ من</span> ("my house")
    - _expected:_ <span dir="rtl">ی</span> (خانه‌ی من)
4. **[morphology_parse]** Break the chain into head + links: <span dir="rtl">دوستِ خوبِ من</span>
    - _expected:_ head <span dir="rtl">دوست</span> + adj <span dir="rtl">خوب</span> (ezafe) + possessor <span dir="rtl">من</span> (ezafe) — "my good friend"

## Review cards

- ezafe → the unstressed <span dir="rtl">-e / -ye</span> linking a noun to its modifiers
- my book → <span dir="rtl">کتابِ من (ketâb-e man)</span>
- University of Tehran → <span dir="rtl">دانشگاهِ تهران (dâneshgâh-e Tehrân)</span>
- my house (after a vowel) → <span dir="rtl">خانه‌ی من (khâne-ye man)</span>
